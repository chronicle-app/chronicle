Add the Apple Wallet plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-wallet`. It extracts the card transactions in Wallet on a Mac, which a card's issuer sends soon after each purchase, with the time to the second, whether the card was used with Apple Pay, in person, or online. Each transaction is a `PayAction` keyed by Wallet's ID for it, with you as its `agent` and the card as its `instrument`. Its `result` is the `Transaction`, which carries the `amount`, the `currency`, and the merchant as its `recipient`, an `Organization` keyed by the name the issuer sends. The card is a `PaymentCard` keyed by the card network's identifier for it, named as its issuer shows it, with its last digits as its `alternateName`, its kind as its `category`, the issuer as its `issuedBy`, and you as its `holder`. The `FinancialAccount` the card draws on is derived from the card, since Wallet has no account number: keyed and named like the card, a `liability` for a credit card and an `asset` for a debit or prepaid one, with the issuer as its `provider`. Each transaction carries that account's side as its one `Posting`, negative for a purchase. Where the transaction happened is its `location` when Wallet knows. Each run is a snapshot, since a pending transaction's amount can change when it posts.

The vocabulary adds `TradeAction` and its kind `PayAction`; the `Transaction` that moving money produces, with its `amount` and `posting` values; the `Posting` that is one account's side of it, with its signed `amount` and its `account`; `MonetaryAmount`, a sum of money with its `value` and `currency`, which every `amount` is; the `FinancialAccount` that money is counted in, with its `accountType`, `provider`, and `holder` values; and the `PaymentCard` that payments draw on, with its `account`, `issuedBy`, and `holder` values. `recipient` applies to a `Transaction`.

`@chronicle.app/etl` adds `reference(node)`: a node's identity alone, its `@type`, `@key`, and the fields its key names, for a node that appears again in one payload. A plugin's `SHAPES.md` leaves out `@asserts`, the completeness marker a snapshot source puts on every node, as it already leaves out `@assertedAt`.
