Add the Apple Wallet plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-wallet`. It extracts the card transactions in Wallet on a Mac, which a card's issuer sends soon after each purchase, with the time to the second, whether the card was used with Apple Pay, in person, or online. Wallet keeps only the ten most recent transactions on each card, so run it often; the archive keeps the rest. Each transaction is a `PayAction` keyed by Wallet's ID for it, with you as its `agent` and the card as its `instrument`. Its `result` is the `FinancialTransaction`, which carries the `amount`, a `MonetaryAmount`, and the merchant as its `recipient`, an `Agent` keyed by the name the issuer sends. The card is a `PaymentCard` keyed by the card network's identifier for it, named as its issuer shows it, with its last digits as its `alternateName` and its kind as its `category`. The `FinancialAccount` the card is `inAccount` is keyed by the issuer's identifier for the account when the issuer gives one, which outlives the card number, and like the card otherwise; it is named after the issuer, as a chart of accounts names it, and is a `liability` for a credit card and an `asset` for a debit or prepaid one. Each transaction carries that account's side as one `AccountEntry` under `hasPart`, negative for a purchase. Where the transaction happened is its `location` when Wallet knows. Each run is a snapshot, since an issuer can update a transaction after sending it.

The vocabulary adds `TradeAction` and its kind `PayAction`; the `FinancialTransaction` that moving money produces, with its `amount`; the `AccountEntry` that is one account's side of it, with its signed `amount`; `MonetaryAmount`, a sum of money with its `value` and `currency`, which every `amount` is; the `FinancialAccount` that money is counted in, with its `accountType`; and the `PaymentCard` that payments draw on. `recipient` applies to a `FinancialTransaction`, and `inAccount` can name a `FinancialAccount`. A property can now name its inverse with `inverseName`: `isPartOf` names `hasPart`, a record can give the relation under either name, the generated types accept the inverse on the forward property's range, and `INVERSE_PROPERTIES` maps each inverse name to the forward property a store keeps.

`@chronicle.app/etl` adds `reference(node)`: a node's identity alone, its `@type`, `@key`, and the fields its key names, for a node that appears again in one payload. A plugin's `SHAPES.md` leaves out `@asserts`, the completeness marker a snapshot source puts on every node, as it already leaves out `@assertedAt`.
