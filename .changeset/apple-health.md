Add the Apple Health plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-health --input export.zip`. It reads the export the Health app makes, zipped or unzipped, and streams its `export.xml`. There are four kinds, grouped as the Health app groups its data, and a bare run asks which to read. Records come out newest first. `heart` has heart rate, resting and walking heart rate, heart rate variability, heart rate recovery, blood pressure, and the rhythm each ECG found. `respiratory` has respiratory rate and blood oxygen. `body-measurements` has sleeping wrist temperature. `sleep` has nights and naps, with their stages. `--series` reads only the series it names, by Health's name for each type, such as `--series RestingHeartRate,RespiratoryRate`. Without a kind, a run reads the kinds they're in. A kind with none of them doesn't read the export. The flag's help lists every series. Each reading is a `MeasureAction` whose `agent` and `object` are you, with the value as a property of you. Its `instrument` is the device by the name it goes by in Health, with its hardware, such as `Watch7,1`, as its `model`, or the app that wrote the value. An ECG names only the watch's model, a `ProductModel` keyed by Apple's identifier for it. Each night or nap is a `SleepAction` whose result is a `SleepSession`, and each of its stages is an `ExperienceAction` whose result is a `Session` that is part of it. The export gives nothing an ID, so each record is keyed by what it says: when it happened, the device or app, and what was measured or the stage. Each record is asserted when Health wrote it, by its samples' `creationDate`.

The vocabulary adds `MeasureAction`, `SleepAction`, and `SleepSession`, and properties of a person for the values measured: `heartRate`, `restingHeartRate`, `walkingHeartRate`, `heartRateVariability`, `heartRateRecovery`, `heartRhythm`, `respiratoryRate`, `oxygenSaturation`, `sleepingWristTemperature`, `systolicBloodPressure`, and `diastolicBloodPressure`. Every one but `heartRhythm` is a metric, marked with two new annotations. `metric` says what form a property's values take, and so how its values over a time combine: a `gauge` is a level or a rate, such as these, and combines as a mean, minimum, or maximum, and a `sum` is an amount, such as a number of steps, and adds up. `unit` is the one unit all its values are in, as a UCUM code such as `/min`, so values from any source can be compared. The generated `METRIC_PROPERTIES` gives each metric's form and unit, and the generator rejects a metric that isn't a gauge or a sum of numbers in one unit. A `Device` can be a watch, and is the instrument of any action done with it. `result` has an inverse, `resultOf`, so a record can name the action it is the result of, and a key can read through it, as a `SleepSession` does for its times.

On the schema site, the keys guide explains paths that follow a property backwards, such as `resultOf.startTime`, and a metric's page gives the form its values take and their unit.
