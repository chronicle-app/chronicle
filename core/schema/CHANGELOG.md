# @chronicle.app/schema

## 0.5.0

### Minor Changes

- 15228bd: Add the Apple Health plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-health --input export.zip`. It reads the export the Health app makes, zipped or unzipped, and streams its `export.xml`. There are five kinds, grouped as the Health app groups its data, and a bare run asks which to read. Records come out newest first. `heart` has heart rate, resting and walking heart rate, heart rate variability, heart rate recovery, blood pressure, and the rhythm each ECG found. `respiratory` has respiratory rate and blood oxygen. `body-measurements` has weight and sleeping wrist temperature. `sleep` has nights and naps, with their stages. `activity` has steps and active energy. `--series` reads only the series it names, by Health's name for each type, such as `--series RestingHeartRate,RespiratoryRate`. Without a kind, a run reads the kinds they're in. A kind with none of them doesn't read the export. The flag's help lists every series. Each reading is a `MeasureAction` whose `agent` and `object` are you, with the value as a property of you. Its `instrument` is the device by the name it goes by in Health, with its hardware, such as `Watch7,1`, as its `model`, or the app that wrote the value. An ECG names only the watch's model, a `ProductModel` keyed by Apple's identifier for it. Each night or nap is a `SleepAction` whose result is a `SleepSession`, and each of its stages is an `ExperienceAction` whose result is a `SleepStage` of it: `light`, `deep`, `REM`, `awake`, or `asleep` when the stage isn't known. Time in bed isn't a stage, and isn't read. The export gives nothing an ID, so each record is keyed by what it says: when it happened, the device or app, and what was measured or the stage. Each record is asserted when Health wrote it, by its samples' `creationDate`.

  The vocabulary adds `MeasureAction`, `SleepAction`, `SleepSession`, and `SleepStage`, and properties of a person for the values measured: `heartRate`, `restingHeartRate`, `walkingHeartRate`, `heartRateVariabilitySdnn`, `heartRateRecovery`, `heartRhythm`, `respiratoryRate`, `oxygenSaturation`, `sleepingWristTemperature`, `weight`, `systolicBloodPressure`, `diastolicBloodPressure`, `stepCount`, and `activeEnergyBurned`. Every one but `heartRhythm` is a metric, marked with two new annotations. `metric` says what form a property's values take, and so how its values over a time combine: a `gauge` is a level or a rate, such as a heart rate, and combines as a mean, minimum, or maximum, and a `sum` is an amount, such as a number of steps or the energy burned, and adds up. `unit` is the one unit all its values are in, as a UCUM code such as `/min`, so values from any source can be compared. The generated `METRIC_PROPERTIES` gives each metric's form and unit, and the generator rejects a metric that isn't a gauge or a sum of numbers in one unit. A `Device` can be a watch, and is the instrument of any action done with it. `result` has an inverse, `resultOf`, so a record can name the action it is the result of, and a key can read through it, as a `SleepSession` does for its times. `isStageOf`, with its inverse `hasStage`, links a session to the session it is a stage of, such as a stretch of deep sleep to its night. It is a kind of `isPartOf`, and a whole's stages of the same type don't overlap.

  On the schema site, the keys guide explains paths that follow a property backwards, such as `resultOf.startTime`, and a metric's page gives the form its values take and their unit.

- da123cb: Add the Apple Notes plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-notes`. It extracts each note in Notes on a Mac, as its last edit and when it was made, and, when asked for with `-t edits`, the bursts of writing still in each one, credited to whoever typed them. A note is an `UpdateAction` at its last edit, whose `object` is the `Note`, keyed by its ID, with its text as Markdown, its hashtags as `tags`, its folder as a `Collection` in the account of whoever owns it, and its image, drawing, PDF, scan, audio, and video attachments under `contains`, and a `CreateAction` by whoever made it. `--since` bounds when each note was last edited, so an incremental run picks up every note changed since. Notes stamps each stretch of text with when it was written and each character with the Notes install that typed it, which each note maps to a person's CloudKit user ID. So `edits` gives each burst of one person's writing as an `UpdateAction` of the note, with its start and end, and the text from it that's still in the note as a `Changeset`, the change it made. You are your Apple Account, as in the other Apple plugins. Anyone else is keyed by their CloudKit user ID in Notes, named from the note's shares, and linked to the same person in other sources by every email and number the shares list, and by the Contacts card each one finds, with its other emails and numbers.

  The vocabulary adds `Note`: a text that its author keeps and edits, such as a note in a notes app or a page in a personal wiki, as against a `DocumentObject`, which is a file. A `Changeset` can be a change applied directly, as the `result` of the `UpdateAction` that made it, as well as a proposal such as a pull request. Its `body` is the text it adds. `nodeKey` gives a node's identity, the hash of the keyset its `@key` names, as a store derives it, so one node can name another, as a note's text names a photo in it as `cid:` and that identity.

- 679e9c8: Add the Apple Photos plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-photos`. It reads your Photos library on a Mac in place, read-only, while Photos runs, and reads what Photos knows about each photo and video rather than the files, most of which iCloud keeps off the Mac. Four kinds:

  - `photos`, the default: each photo and video, as what you did to bring it into the library. A camera photo is a `PhotographAction` and a video a `RecordAction`, dated when taken, with the kind of camera as a `ProductModel`, keyed by its make and model in the `exif` namespace. A screenshot is a `ScreenshotAction`, a screen recording a `RecordAction`, and a Live Photo taken during a FaceTime call a `PhotographAction` with FaceTime as its `instrument`. A photo an app saved, such as from WhatsApp or Messages, is an `AddAction` dated when it was added, with the app as its `instrument`. Each action is keyed by the work it made or added: the photo as a `StillImage` or the video as a `MovingImage`, keyed by iCloud's ID for it, with its title, caption, keywords, the people whose faces you named in it as `depicts`, its albums and their folders as `isPartOf`, and the camera's mode it was taken in, such as Portrait or slo-mo, as `sourceFormat`. Each of its original files is a `rendition`, an `ImageObject` or a `VideoObject` keyed by iCloud's fingerprint of the file, with its original file name, size in bytes as `contentSize`, dimensions, type, and length, and a photo's lens and exposure as `exifData`, by EXIF's tag names. A Live Photo has two: its image and its clip. Where it was taken is the action's `location`, or the work's own when an app saved it, since someone else took it. What Photos works out for itself is left out: its model's captions, its search labels, the address it looks up, its guess at which person is you, and the faces it matches to people. So are photos you hid, and photos someone else added to an iCloud Shared Library.
  - `activity`: what you did with your photos, each dated: views, shares, and edits, and from the library's change log, likes and unlikes, caption and title changes, and adds to albums. What follows a photo from iCloud when it arrives, such as after the library is rebuilt, isn't something you did then, and is left out. The change log's bits are looked up by property name in the library's own Core Data model, since they move between macOS versions.
  - `favorites`: your favorites the change log has no date for, from before it begins or that arrived with the photo, each a `LikeAction` with no `timestamp`, keyed by the work.
  - `shared-with-you`: the photos and videos shared with you in Messages, from the library Photos keeps of them, each a `ShareAction` of a work keyed by Messages' attachment ID, crediting who sent it from Messages and dated when they sent it. The work's `rendition` is the attachment as the iMessage plugin keys it. A photo shared in a group chat `target`s the group's `Channel`, keyed as the iMessage plugin keys it.

  A photo or video's work links into Photos by its key, with a `deepLinks` template that opens Photos with it selected.

  `chronicle scan` finds the library in `~/Pictures`, and a scan of a folder finds a library's `Photos.sqlite` by the tables the plugin declares it reads. Extract refuses a database that isn't a Photos library, or one from a Photos without a column it reads, and `chronicle sources info` says it needs Full Disk Access. `chronicle survey` counts each kind, and when, without extracting it: photos and Shared with You from their rows alone, and activity and favorites from the change log, as a run reads it.

  `@chronicle.app/etl-sqlite` adds `assertDatabaseSchema`, the check a `SqliteExtractor` makes of its declared schema, for a reader that opens its database itself.

  The vocabulary adds `ScreenshotAction`; `UnlikeAction`, which withdraws a like; `likes`, the entities an agent likes, which a `LikeAction` adds to and an `UnlikeAction` removes from, declared with the new `assertedBy`, `retractedBy`, and `runPredicate` annotations; `ShareAction`, which maps to schema.org's; `depicts`, something that appears in a picture or video, such as a person; `exifData`, what an image's file records about how it was taken, as an object of EXIF tags and their values, in a new `JSONObject` datatype; `StillImage` and `MovingImage`, a picture or a video as a work, apart from the files that hold it; and `rendition`, a file that holds a work, such as a picture's image file and the short video captured with it, or an episode's audio, which maps to schema.org's `encoding`; and `contentSize`, a media file's size in bytes. `contains` is a file that's part of an entity, such as a message's attachment. `caption` applies to a `StillImage` and a `MovingImage` as well as an `ImageObject`.

- eea5dd3: Add the Apple Podcasts plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-podcasts`. It extracts the episodes you played or saved in Podcasts on a Mac, and the shows you follow. A played episode is a `ListenAction`, and a finished one also a `CompleteAction`, dated when it was last played; a saved episode is a `BookmarkAction`; and a followed show is a `FollowAction`, dated when it was added to the library. Each is keyed by the episode or show it acted on, which is keyed by Apple's catalog ID for it, the same on each device, and an episode or show links to its page on Apple Podcasts and `references` the page its feed links it to, with you as its `agent`; what isn't in the catalog is left out, and the run says how many. The episode is a `PodcastEpisode` with its show as its `series`, when it was released as its `datePublished`, its audio file as its `rendition`, its artwork, its description as Markdown, and its `transcript` when the app has downloaded one. The author a feed names for a show or an episode is its `author`, an `Agent` by that name, and a show's channel on Apple Podcasts is its `publisher`, keyed by the channel's catalog ID. Without an Apple Account signed in to the Mac, extraction stops and says to sign in. Each run is a snapshot, since the library keeps only each episode's latest state.

  The vocabulary adds `PodcastSeries`, a podcast; `webFeed`, the RSS or Atom feeds that publish a work's items, such as a podcast's episodes or a blog's posts; and `series`, the series a work is one of, such as the podcast an episode belongs to.

- 8c47a8f: Add the Apple Wallet plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-wallet`. It extracts the card transactions in Wallet on a Mac, which a card's issuer sends soon after each purchase, with the time to the second, whether the card was used with Apple Pay, in person, or online. Wallet keeps only the ten most recent transactions on each card, so run it often; the archive keeps the rest. Each transaction is a `PayAction`, with you as its `agent` and the card as its `instrument`. Its `result` is the `FinancialTransaction`, keyed by Wallet's ID for it, and the action is keyed by its transaction. The transaction carries the `amount`, a `MonetaryAmount`, and the merchant as its `recipient`, an `Agent` keyed by the name the issuer sends. The card is a `PaymentCard` keyed by the card network's identifier for it, named as its issuer shows it, with its last digits as its `alternateName` and its kind as its `category`. The `FinancialAccount` the card is `inAccount` is keyed by the issuer's identifier for the account when the issuer gives one, which outlives the card number, and otherwise by the card the payment used; it is named after the issuer, as a chart of accounts names it, and is a `liability` for a credit card and an `asset` for a debit or prepaid one. Each transaction carries that account's side as one `FinancialPosting` under `hasPart`, keyed by the transaction and the account, negative for a purchase. Where the transaction happened is its `location` when Wallet knows. Each run is a snapshot, since an issuer can update a transaction after sending it.

  The vocabulary adds `TradeAction` and its kind `PayAction`; the `FinancialTransaction` that moving money produces, with its `amount`; the `FinancialPosting` that is one account's side of it, with its signed `amount`; `MonetaryAmount`, a sum of money with its `value` and `currency`, which every `amount` is; the `FinancialAccount` that money is counted in, with its `financialAccountType`; and the `PaymentCard` that payments draw on. `recipient` applies to a `FinancialTransaction`, and `inAccount` can name a `FinancialAccount`. A property can now name its inverse with `inverseName`: `isPartOf` names `hasPart`, a record can give the relation under either name, the generated types accept the inverse on the forward property's range, and `INVERSE_PROPERTIES` maps each inverse name to the forward property a store keeps.

  `@chronicle.app/etl` adds `reference(node)`: a node's identity alone, its `@type`, `@key`, and the fields its key names, for a node that appears again in one payload. A plugin's `SHAPES.md` leaves out `@asserts`, the completeness marker a snapshot source puts on every node, as it already leaves out `@assertedAt`.

- 93266b0: A `Device`'s `model` says what kind of device it is, and a device has no `category` for it. Timing's devices no longer have a `category` such as `computer` or `phone`. Their `model`, such as `Mac15,3`, already says what they are.
- b112f78: An iMessage `MessageAction` has its `Message` as its `result`, not its `object`, since sending a message brings it into being, as a call's session is its `CallAction`'s `result`. The vocabulary's `MessageAction` says so, and its examples and the schema site's guides show it.

  iMessage records are events, so a later run adds to what an earlier one stated and doesn't replace it. A store that kept iMessage records from an earlier release has each message as its action's `object` and its `result` once iMessage runs again. Drop those records and extract iMessage again to keep only the `result`.

  A run's progress line names an action by its `result` when it has no `object`, so an iMessage run shows each message's text rather than `MessageAction`, and a Photos run each photo's title or file name.

- a2160b5: A `Session` is one person's time, such as a night's sleep or their time on a call. Others who took part have sessions of their own, and something they shared, such as a concert or a meeting, is an `Event` that each session is part of. A `CallSession` is one person's time on a call. A `Journey` is a `Session`, so a trip has a planned period and stages like any other session.

### Patch Changes

- d69e45f: An ID names one thing. An action that a source gives no ID of its own no longer takes the ID of what it acted on or made as its `sourceId`, which gave two nodes one ID. It is keyed through that node instead, by `object.sourceId` or `result.sourceId`, and also by its time where it can happen more than once.

  An action is keyed by what it acted on: a Things action by its task, a Google Calendar plan by its event, an opened GitHub issue by the issue, a Google Reader read, share, or star by the item and you, a Twitter like by the tweet, a Last.fm or YouTube follow by the account it follows, a Zotero save by the item, and a Zotero view by the item and when.

  An action that makes something has it as its `result` and is keyed by it. Every `MessageAction` has its `Message` as its `result`, as iMessage's does: in Claude Code, Email, Gmail, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Slack, Twitter, and WhatsApp too. A `PublishAction` has the work it published as its `result`: a Bluesky post, an Instagram post, a tweet, a YouTube video, a GitHub pull request or gist, and a Hacker News submission. A call is keyed by its session, a voice memo by its recording, an Arc trip by its journey, and a Timing block or entry by its session. Highlighting is keyed by the quotation it made, in Marvin and Zotero, and commenting by the note or comment it made, in Marvin, Zotero, YouTube, Hacker News, and GitHub. Creating a GitHub repository and committing are keyed by the repository and the commit, creating an Are.na channel, block, or comment by what it made, rather than by IDs made up as `channel-11`, and registering a Last.fm account by the account.

  Nodes that shared an ID are keyed apart. An Instagram post, which the export gives no ID of its own, is keyed by the files it contains rather than by its first file's ID. A FoodNoms entry's ID is its action's, still without `@type`, so the eat-or-drink guess can change, and its `Meal` is keyed by the action it was eaten in. A comment on a Marvin or Zotero highlight, which shares the highlight's ID, is keyed by the quotation it is about. A WhatsApp attachment is keyed by its message. A Google Calendar event that names no one, such as a holiday or a subscribed feed's, has no agent, rather than its calendar standing in as an `Agent` with the calendar's ID.

  Google Reader's Share is a `ShareAction` of the item rather than a `PublishAction`, since it passed along an article someone else wrote. The vocabulary's `PublishAction` says its `result` is the work, and its examples show these keys. The schema site's keys guide and the plugin scaffold say how to key an action without an ID of its own.

  Records these plugins extracted before this release were keyed differently, so extracting them again adds new nodes beside the old ones, and the old messages and publications stay as their actions' `object`. Drop these plugins' records and extract them again.

## 0.4.0

### Minor Changes

- b0982a5: Add the Chrome plugin. It reads browsing history from a Chrome profile's `History` database, including visits synced from other devices, and emits a `ViewAction` for each page visit. The agent is the profile's Google account, by its email address, as Gmail and Google Calendar know you. A visit reached by following a link or submitting a form also becomes a `NavigateAction` from the page it came from to the page it landed on, and that page `references` the link followed. The vocabulary adds `MoveAction` and `NavigateAction`. `@chronicle.app/etl-sqlite` adds `chromeToUnixMsSql` and `unixMsToChromeTimestamp` for Chromium's microseconds since 1601. `renderShapes` shows each further action a record becomes as its own node.
- ac25170: Add the Apple Voice Memos plugin, run as `chronicle extract apple-voice-memos`. It extracts the recordings in the Voice Memos library on a Mac, which syncs through iCloud and so holds memos from every device on the account. Each memo is a `RecordAction` dated when recording started, and its `result` is the recording, an `AudioObject` keyed by the memo's ID, with its title, duration, and file. Apple's transcript, when the memo has one, is the recording's `transcript`. The action's `instrument` is what the audio file says recorded it: the OS release, an `OperatingSystem` as the file writes it, and the kind of device, a `ProductModel` keyed by its Wikidata ID, but never which device. Memos in Recently Deleted are left out.

  The vocabulary adds two kinds of `CreateAction`, `RecordAction` for recording sound or video and `PhotographAction` for taking a photograph, and `transcript`, the words spoken in an audio or video recording or a podcast episode, written out as text. `Software` is the new parent of `SoftwareApplication`, `OperatingSystem` (an operating system or one release of it), and `AIModel` (a trained model, such as a language model).

- f0a3390: `DrankAction` is now `DrinkAction`, in the present tense like the other actions and named like schema.org's `DrinkAction`. FoodNoms emits a `DrinkAction` for each drink.
- b567d4e: `DateTime` is now the one datatype for a point in time, and `Date` is gone. A `DateTime` is a JavaScript `Date` or an EDTF string at the precision the source knows: a UTC instant (`2024-03-02T14:05:00Z`), a year, month, or day (`1987`, `1987-06`, `1987-06-12`), unspecified digits (`198X`, `XXXX-03-12`), and an uncertain or approximate date (`1987?`, `1950~`). Strings are checked rather than accepted as they are, and `isDateTime` checks one. `@assertedAt` must be an instant, which `isInstant` checks, so an event dated only partly is no longer asserted at that date. FoodNoms timestamps are now instants rather than strings with a space before the time.
- 61ad43e: A GitHub plugin reads your pull requests, issues, commits, repositories, how issues and pull requests were resolved, comments, stars, and gists through the GitHub GraphQL API, plus other people's replies to you. An issue is a `Task` (opened with a `PlanAction`, closed with a `CompleteAction` or `CancelAction`); a pull request is a `Changeset` (opened with a `PublishAction`, merged with an `AcceptAction`, declined with a `RejectAction`, or withdrawn with a `CancelAction`). It signs in with `--token`, a token stored with `chronicle auth set github`, `GH_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_TOKEN`, or the gh CLI's login. A commit is a `Revision`, keyed by its hash across sources and `isBasedOn` its parents. The vocabulary adds `SoftwareSourceCode`, `Repository`, `Changeset`, `Revision`, `AcceptAction`, `RejectAction`, and `isBasedOn`.
- ee0525c: You are one node across Google sources: your address as an `email` Agent, `sameAs` `@me` and your Google account, an Agent keyed by the same address in the `google-account` namespace (`googleAccount(address)` in `@chronicle.app/google`). The vocabulary adds `inAccount`: the agent whose account holds an entity, as that account's service knows them, which an entity held in several accounts has for each, and whose own identifiers are unique within it. A Gmail message is `inAccount` the account at the mailbox's address (`emailAccount(address)` in `@chronicle.app/email-core`), as any mail source of that mailbox can say. A Gmail thread is `inAccount` the Google account, which issued its ID, and keyed within it. Gmail's own message IDs are no longer kept: the Message-ID identifies the message. Gmail's thread links name their account with `authuser`, so they open in the right one, and there's no per-message link. `messageAction` takes the `account` holding a message and `meIdentities`, linked from every agent that's the owner, and writes the author by key, since the action's agent is the same person in full. A Google account's own calendar, whose ID is its address, is `inAccount` that account, so a calendar shared with you is its owner's; other calendars don't say whose they are. A calendar event is now keyed by its iCalendar UID, as the planning is, and `sameAs` each calendar's copy by its `eid`, as a Gmail message is keyed by its Message-ID. Google Reader keys its account link by address instead of user ID. In Google Calendar, you are the signed-in account's address wherever it appears, or a guest marked as your primary calendar's owner, not the owner of a calendar shared with you. Every event's agent is who put it on the calendar: the organizer, or the creator when the organizer is a calendar, else the calendar's owner or the calendar itself. Calendars are a new `Calendar`, a kind of `Collection`. The vocabulary adds `alternateName`, another name an entity was shown with. A display name in a mail header or an invitation is now the address's `alternateName`, not its `name`: it's what one message called it, and a group or notification address carries a different one in nearly every message. A calendar has a `name` only when its summary isn't its ID. With `--link-contacts`, a person on one of your contacts is `sameAs` the contact itself, keyed by its ID within your Google account (`google-contacts`), named as you saved it, and `sameAs` its other addresses and numbers.
- 3b3a921: Google sources sign in with `chronicle auth login google`, using an OAuth client you make in your own Google Cloud project. The first sign-in walks you through it: gcloud (in a configuration of Chronicle's own) creates the project and turns the APIs on, the Cloud console pages for the consent screen and the client open one at a time, and you paste in the client's ID and secret. Chronicle signs out of gcloud when setup is done. Each account you sign in with is kept apart; `--account` picks one, and `--add drive` asks for more access. A Google Calendar plugin reads your calendars' events, newest first by when they were put on a calendar, as `PlanAction`s keyed by iCalendar UID (in the `icalendar` namespace) on each calendar's copy of the `Event`, keyed by Google's ID for it, linked into Google Calendar with `deepLinks`, and `sameAs` the UID, with their guests as `attendee`s keyed by email address as the mail plugins key people, and descriptions converted from HTML to Markdown. With `--link-contacts`, Gmail and Calendar use your Google Contacts as a lookup: a person on one of your contacts is `sameAs` the contact's other addresses and phone numbers. It's off by default; `--add contacts` adds the access. The vocabulary adds `attendee`, and `scheduledStart` and `scheduledEnd` for when an event or session is planned; `startTime` and `endTime` are for actions only, and the unused `Interval` is removed. `involvement` is abstract, a family to query by rather than a property to set, and `recipient` joins `member` and `attendee` in it.
- c7d87c5: **Breaking:** an action's `instrument` is now a list, and a single value no longer validates. A plugin built against an earlier release that gives one instrument, such as Timing or Claude Code before this release, fails validation until it gives a list. An action can now name several things it used, each as precisely as its source knows: the software that ran, the kind of device it ran on, and the particular device once a source tells which one. The vocabulary adds `ProductModel`, a kind of product such as the iPhone line, for a source that knows only the kind of device. Timing's `ExecuteAction` and Claude Code's `MessageAction` give their instrument as a list of one, and Claude Code's model is an `AIModel` rather than a `SoftwareApplication`.
- 04cce7e: `chronicle.ttl` is now an OWL ontology with SHACL shapes. Classes are `owl:Class`, and properties are `owl:ObjectProperty` or `owl:DatatypeProperty` with an `rdfs:domain` and `rdfs:range`, written as an `owl:unionOf` when there are several. A property that takes one value has a SHACL shape with `sh:maxCount 1`, in place of `owl:maxCardinality` on the property. `:domainIncludes` and `:rangeIncludes` are gone. `Text`, `URL`, `DateTime`, and `Number` are datatypes defined over XSD, `URL` is no longer a kind of `Text`, and `DataType` is gone. `:Base` is the disjoint union of `:Entity` and `:Action`. The generated types and validators are unchanged, and a class with two parents now generates.
- 243db5a: The vocabulary points each term to the closest schema.org term with `skos:exactMatch`, `skos:closeMatch`, or `skos:broadMatch`, as a hint for mapping data. A reasoner draws nothing from these, so the vocabulary stands on its own. The four `owl:equivalentClass` and two `owl:equivalentProperty` links to schema.org are now `skos:exactMatch`. Generated types are unchanged. The schema site shows how each term relates to schema.org and Activity Streams 2.0, with notes on how they differ, and has a page comparing Chronicle with each vocabulary. The build checks every mapping against the terms of a pinned release of each vocabulary, and fails on a subclass or an equivalence that points to another vocabulary.

### Patch Changes

- 3b3a921: A `body` or `description` is plain text or Markdown, never HTML. `tidyText` cleans text a source wrote for a screen: it removes padding characters and takes tracking parameters off links, and with `dropLongUrls` (for bulk mail) drops click-tracking redirect URLs while keeping the link's words. `sampleTransform` fails a plugin's SHAPES test when a `body` or `description` holds HTML; a plugin whose text is code passes `allowMarkup`.

## 0.3.0

The vocabulary version (`owl:versionInfo`, exported as `SCHEMA_VERSION`) is now `0.2.0`. It adds 119 terms since the vocabulary released with 0.2.0 and removes none.

### Minor Changes

- d8d36ef: Add the Arc Timeline plugin, bundled with the CLI. It reads Arc Timeline's daily iCloud backup and emits a `VisitAction` for each stay and a `TravelAction` with a `Journey` for each trip. The vocabulary adds `VisitAction`, `TravelAction`, `Journey`, `Place`, `Venue`, the `Location` structured value, and `Number`, with `startTime`, `endTime`, `result`, `location`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `address`, `category`, `travelMode`, `distance`, and `path`.
- 3431450: Add the Are.na plugin, bundled with the CLI. It reads your channels, blocks, connections, comments, and follows through the Are.na API. The vocabulary adds `caption` for image alt text.
- 65ddcca: Add the Call History plugin, bundled with the CLI. It reads phone and FaceTime calls from the macOS Call History database and emits a `CallAction` whose result is a `CallSession` for each call. The vocabulary adds `InteractAction`, `CommunicateAction`, `CallAction`, `Session`, and `CallSession`, and `recipient` applies to `CallSession`.
- 80598fa: Add the Facebook plugin, bundled with the CLI.
