# @chronicle.app/apple-photos

Your Photos library on a Mac: each photo and video, who you said is in it, and what you did with it, and the photos shared with you in Messages, with who sent them.

## Usage

```sh
chronicle extract apple-photos
chronicle extract apple-photos activity --since 2026-01-01
chronicle extract apple-photos photos activity favorites shared-with-you
chronicle extract apple-photos --input "/path/to/Photos Library.photoslibrary"
```

The library is `~/Pictures/Photos Library.photoslibrary`, and the plugin reads its `database/Photos.sqlite` in place, read-only, with built-in `node:sqlite`, while Photos runs. Your terminal needs Full Disk Access.

It reads what Photos knows about each photo, not the photo itself. Most originals in an iCloud library aren't on the Mac, so no file is read or downloaded, and no record has a `contentPath`.

| Record type       | Default | What it is                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `photos`          | yes     | One record for each photo and video in your library                                                 |
| `activity`        | no      | Views, shares, edits, caption and title changes, likes and unlikes, and adds to albums              |
| `favorites`       | no      | Your favorites that have no date, one like each                                                     |
| `shared-with-you` | no      | The photos and videos shared with you in Messages, with who sent them, from `--syndication` (below) |

Each kind comes out newest first, and `--since` and `--until` bound when each record happened.

`shared-with-you` reads the library Photos keeps of what was shared with you in Messages, `~/Library/Photos/Libraries/Syndication.photoslibrary` (`--syndication`), and Messages' `~/Library/Messages/chat.db` (`--messages`) for who sent each one and the chat it came in.

### Decisions

**What you did to make each photo.** A photo from a camera is a `PhotographAction` and a video a `RecordAction`, with the photo as its `result`, dated when it was taken. A screenshot is a `ScreenshotAction`, and a screen recording a `RecordAction`. A Live Photo taken during a FaceTime call is a `RecordAction` whose `result` is the clip, which `contains` the still, with FaceTime as its `instrument`. Anything else an app added, such as a photo saved from WhatsApp, Instagram, or Messages, or AirDropped, is an `AddAction` with the photo as its `object`, dated when it was added, and the app as its `instrument` when Photos names one.

**Where it was taken.** A photo you took was taken where you were, so its position is the action's `location`. A photo an app saved was taken by someone, somewhere, before you saved it, so its position is the photo's own `location`, not the save's. A Shared with You photo's is the photo's too.

- The test for a camera photo is strict: the Camera app added it, or no app did, and its file names a camera. A photo of yours saved from Instagram is a save, which the Instagram plugin can claim.
- Screenshots and screen recordings are told by their kind first, since many have no app.
- A photo with no app and no camera data is a save. Most have no date in their file, and the video among them is mostly what Messages compresses to.

**When it was taken is Photos' date.** The date is `ZDATECREATED`, a UTC instant, exactly as Photos has it, including a date you changed in Photos. A photo whose file has no date has the date Photos gave it, usually when the file was saved.

**Keys are iCloud's IDs.** A photo is keyed by iCloud's ID for it (`ZCLOUDASSETGUID`), an album by its own (`ZCLOUDGUID`), and a person by Photos' ID for them (`ZPERSONUUID`). They stay the same when the library is rebuilt from iCloud and on every Mac on the same library, while the library's own IDs (`ZUUID`) don't. A photo that isn't in iCloud is keyed by the library's ID.

**A photo links into Photos by its key.** The plugin declares a `deepLinks` template for `ImageObject` and `VideoObject`, `photos://asset?identifier=<key>&cloud-identifier=<key>:001`, which opens Photos with the photo selected. Photos finds a photo by iCloud's ID in `cloud-identifier`, with the `:001` it gives every photo's ID, and by the library's ID in `identifier`, so one template covers a photo that isn't in iCloud too. Photos doesn't document the link. A Shared with You photo is keyed by Messages' ID, which Photos doesn't know it by, so its link doesn't open it.

**The kind of camera, not which camera.** A camera photo's `instrument` is a `ProductModel` named by the make and model its file gives, such as `Apple iPhone 14 Pro`, keyed by its type and that name in the `exif` namespace, since it has no ID, so other sources reading the same files name the same one. A name isn't an ID the way Apple's model identifier is, so the key keeps its type, and a lens or a device of the same name stays apart from it. Photos can't tell two phones of one model apart, so there's no `Device`.

**People you named in it.** A photo `depicts` each person whose face in it you named: on its own, in a group of faces Photos showed you, or in iPhoto or Aperture, or a face you added yourself. Photos records how each face came to be its person's (`ZDETECTEDFACE.ZNAMESOURCE`, with `ZMANUAL` for a face you added), and most are its own matches, which are left out. A person merged into another is the one they were merged into. Pets, people you haven't named, and where faces are in the photo are left out. Few named people link to a contact, so they don't merge with contacts by themselves.

**The camera's mode it was taken in.** A photo or video taken in one of the camera's modes has it as its `sourceFormat`, as Photos' Media Types name them: `livePhoto`, `portrait`, `panorama`, `burst`, `sloMo`, `timeLapse`, or `cinematic`. A Portrait or a burst that's also a Live Photo is the Portrait or the burst. A screenshot and a screen recording are told apart by their action instead.

**The lens and the exposure, as EXIF tags.** A photo's `exifData` is what its file records about how it was taken, as Photos keeps it, by EXIF 3.0's tag names: `LensModel`, `FocalLength`, `FocalLengthIn35mmFilm`, `FNumber`, `ExposureTime`, `PhotographicSensitivity`, `ExposureBiasValue`, `MeteringMode`, `WhiteBalance`, `Flash`, `DigitalZoomRatio`, and `Orientation`. Each value is EXIF's: a fraction as a decimal, to six significant digits, and a code as its number. Photos keeps only whether the flash fired, so `Flash` is `1` or `0`. It's kept only for a photo whose file records its exposure, and so is an upright `Orientation` (`1`), since Photos records both for a screenshot too. A video's settings come from its own metadata, not EXIF, so a video has none. Photos' Photographic Styles and why it took a photo aren't EXIF, so they're left out. The make and model are the camera's `name`.

**Only what you and your camera recorded.** What Photos works out for itself is left out: the captions its model writes, the labels its search shows, the address it looks up for where a photo was taken, its guess at which person is you, and the faces it matches to people.

**Albums are what you made.** A photo `isPartOf` each album you made that it's in, and an album `isPartOf` its folder. Recently Deleted, shared albums, Memories, and the albums Photos makes are left out. Each run reads the library as it is now, so an album, keyword, or person taken off a photo closes while the photo has others. Taking off its last one doesn't close it: listing them in `@asserts` would, at the cost of a marker on every photo that has none, and few photos have any.

**What's left out.** Photos in Recently Deleted, the burst photos Photos hides, photos you hid, and photos in iCloud shared albums are left out, and so are Memories and the albums Photos makes. A photo in an iCloud Shared Library is yours when you added it: Photos records who added each one, and a photo someone else added is left out, since you didn't take or save it.

**What you did with a photo is dated.** Photos keeps only when a photo was last viewed, shared, and edited, so `activity` records each as it sees it, and history builds up over runs. The rest comes from the library's change log, Core Data's persistent history, which goes back as far as Photos has kept it:

- A photo's favorite flag changed. The last change made it what it is now, and each change before turned it the other way, so a favorite is a `LikeAction`, and one you removed an `UnlikeAction`.
- Its caption or title changed: an `UpdateAction`. A caption that arrived with the photo, such as on import, isn't an edit, and nor is a caption's row Photos adds with nothing in it.
- It was added to an album: an `AddAction` with the album as its `target`. Adding photos to an album changes both the album and each photo, in one transaction. A photo added when several albums changed was added to whichever of them it's in now. A photo in none of them gets no add, and taking a photo out of an album isn't recorded, since the photo's snapshot closes it.

A photo that was in your library before this Mac had it, such as when the library is rebuilt from iCloud, arrives first, and its albums, favorite, caption, and title follow it over the next hour or so, each in a change of its own. So a change to such a photo within a day of it arriving is left out: it's the photo arriving, not something you did then. Its favorite then has no date, so it's in `favorites`.

A change made on this Mac is dated when it was made. One made on another device is dated when this Mac received it from iCloud: usually soon after, but later if the Mac was asleep.

**The change log is read by property name.** Each change has a bitmask of the properties it changed, and Core Data numbers them per entity, differently in each macOS version. The plugin reads the library's own model (`Z_MODELCACHE`) and lays the properties out as Core Data does, so the bits are looked up by name: a photo's `favorite` and `albums`, an album's `assets`, a caption's `longDescription`, and a title's `title`. A model without one of them, such as after a macOS release renames it, can't be read, and `activity` says so. Entity numbers come from `Z_PRIMARYKEY`, and columns from the tables themselves.

**A favorite with no date is a like with no date.** A favorite from before the change log begins, or one that arrived with its photo, has no date, so `favorites` gives each as its own `LikeAction` with no `timestamp`, keyed by the photo alone, and read as of when the library last changed. Removing one is a change the log dates, so it's an `UnlikeAction` in `activity`, which takes the photo out of your `likes`. An unchanged library gives the same records on every run.

**Shared with You.** The photos and videos shared with you in Messages are in a library of their own. Each is a `ShareAction` keyed by Messages' ID for the attachment, which Photos keeps (`ZSYNDICATIONIDENTIFIER`), and its photo is keyed by the same ID, with `sameAs` the attachment as the `imessage` plugin keys it, so what Photos has for it lands on the iMessage attachment. Who sent it comes from Messages: a `Person` keyed by their handle, `sameAs` Messages' participant. So does when: each is dated when its message was sent, since Photos dates a photo when it added it to the library, which is when the library was last rebuilt. Who it was sent to is left to the message, which the `imessage` plugin records with its recipients. A photo shared in a group chat `target`s the group's `Channel`, keyed by Apple's ID for the group, as the `imessage` plugin keys it. Messages says which chat it came in, since Photos doesn't record whether a conversation is a group. A one-to-one chat has no `Channel`, so a photo shared in one has no `target`, and nor does one shared in a group in an older Messages, which keeps no group IDs. Photos with camera data, videos, and screenshots are included. Other images, mostly stickers and images from the web, are left out, and so is a photo whose message is gone from Messages. So is a photo you sent: Photos keeps it as a copy with no link to the photo in your library, and `activity` already has your share of that photo.

**The agent is your iCloud account,** whose library this is, from `@chronicle.app/apple`. It's omitted when no account can be read. An explicit `account` object (or null) describes a copied library without a host account lookup.

## Tests

The tests build a synthetic Photos library, with its own small Core Data model and change log, a Shared with You library, and a Messages database with `node:sqlite`, and pass an explicit iCloud account. They never read the host's Photos library, Messages, or iCloud account.
