# @chronicle.app/apple-health

Your heart, respiratory, and body readings, including your weight, your steps and active energy, and your sleep, from the export the Health app makes.

## Usage

In the Health app on your iPhone, tap your picture, then Export All Health Data. Pass the `export.zip` it makes:

```sh
chronicle extract apple-health --input ~/Downloads/export.zip
chronicle extract apple-health sleep --input ~/Downloads/export.zip --since 2024-01-01
chronicle extract apple-health --series RestingHeartRate --input ~/Downloads/export.zip
chronicle extract apple-health all --input ~/Downloads/export.zip --limit 0 --stream --output health.json
```

`--input` also takes the `apple_health_export` folder it unzips to, a folder that holds either, or `export.xml`. A zip is read in place, never unpacked to disk.

There are five kinds, grouped as the Health app groups its data. Apple Health declares no default kinds: a bare `chronicle extract apple-health` asks which, and outside a terminal says to name them. `all` reads every kind. Each kind reads these series:

| Kind                | Series                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `heart`             | `HeartRate`, `RestingHeartRate`, `WalkingHeartRateAverage`, `HeartRateVariabilitySDNN`, `HeartRateRecoveryOneMinute`, `BloodPressure`, and `Electrocardiogram`, the rhythm each ECG found |
| `respiratory`       | `RespiratoryRate`, `OxygenSaturation`                                                                                                                                                     |
| `body-measurements` | `BodyMass` (your weight), `AppleSleepingWristTemperature`                                                                                                                                 |
| `activity`          | `StepCount`, `ActiveEnergyBurned`                                                                                                                                                         |
| `sleep`             | `SleepAnalysis`: nights and naps, with their sleep stages                                                                                                                                 |

`--series` reads only the series it names, comma-separated, such as `--series RestingHeartRate,RespiratoryRate`. A run that names no kinds reads the kinds they're in. One that names kinds reads the series among them, and says which kinds the series ask for that it leaves out. A series is Health's name for a type without HealthKit's prefix, in any case. `chronicle extract apple-health --help` lists them, and so does the error for a name that isn't one.

`export.xml` holds everything Health keeps and can be several gigabytes. It's streamed, and each kind reads it once, or not at all when the run reads none of its series from it. A run that reads it also reads it once more to learn which sources are devices. Records come out newest first. The file keeps no useful order, so each kind holds what it reads, in a compact form, until it has read all of it. A whole export can be a million readings or more, so use `--stream` to write each record as it comes rather than holding every record until the end. `--since` and `--until` bound when each reading or sleep period started, and `--limit` keeps the newest.

| The value        | a property of you     |                                                                                                                                  | `heartRate`, `restingHeartRate`, `walkingHeartRate`, `heartRateVariabilitySdnn`, `heartRateRecovery`, `heartRhythm`, `respiratoryRate`, `oxygenSaturation`, `sleepingWristTemperature`, `weight`, `systolicBloodPressure`, `diastolicBloodPressure`, `stepCount`, `activeEnergyBurned` |

`SHAPES.md` sketches the output, generated from the tests.

### Decisions

**A reading is a `MeasureAction` on you.** You're its `agent` and its `object`, and the value measured is a property of you, such as your `heartRate`. Each value property is a metric in the vocabulary, with the unit its values are in: a gauge, a level or a rate such as a heart rate, or a sum, an amount such as a number of steps. A reading taken at an instant has a `timestamp`. One over a period, such as a day's resting heart rate or the steps taken in a few minutes, has a `startTime` and `endTime`.

**Records are keyed by what they say.** The export gives samples no IDs, and Chronicle doesn't make them up, so a reading is keyed by its own fields: when it was taken, the names of its instruments (`instrument[*].handle`: the device or app), and the value it measured, by the property it's on, such as `object.heartRate`. A sample the export repeats exactly has the same key. Two different values from one device at the same instant are two readings. A reading a new export repeats keeps its key. Health condenses older samples, though, so a later export can have one reading over a stretch of time where an earlier one had many, under a new key, and no longer have those. A night, and each of its stages, is keyed by its times and its source, and a stage also by the stage.

**A record is asserted when Health wrote it.** Each sample says when Health wrote it, its `creationDate`, and that's its record's `@assertedAt`, rather than when the reading was taken. A day's resting heart rate, for one, is written the next morning. A night is asserted when the last of its samples was written. A condensed reading was written when Health condensed it, so it's asserted later than the readings it replaces. An ECG names no such time and is asserted when it was recorded.

**The instrument is the device or the app.** A device is keyed by the name it goes by in Health, such as `Sam’s Apple Watch`, with its hardware as its `model`, such as `Watch7,1`. A renamed device is a second device. A source that never describes a device, such as Sleep Cycle, or values you entered in Health, is a `SoftwareApplication` named by its name. Some samples, such as sleep and resting heart rate, don't describe their device. They take its model from what the rest of the export says about the source. An ECG names only the watch's model, so its instrument is a `ProductModel` keyed by Apple's identifier for it. The OS release a sample names isn't kept.

**Times are instants.** Every date in `export.xml` carries the UTC offset in effect when the export was made, not where the sample was taken: an export made in summer writes winter samples with a summer offset. The instant is right, and that's what's kept. Sleep and some other samples name their time zone, such as `America/Toronto`. The extracted record keeps it, but the vocabulary has no term for it yet.

**Sleep is grouped into periods, each source on its own.** A source's sleep samples that overlap or fall within two hours of each other are one period, such as a night or a nap. Each period is a `SleepAction` whose result is a `SleepSession`. Each sample in it is a stage: an `ExperienceAction` whose result is a `Session` that `isPartOf` the period's `SleepSession`, with the stage as its `category`: `in bed`, `asleep`, `core`, `deep`, `REM`, or `awake`. Sources aren't merged. A phone's time in bed and a watch's stages for the same night are two nights, told apart by their instrument. Time in each stage is the sum of its stages, so no totals are stored. A session has no times of its own, so its key reads them from the action it's the result of, through `resultOf`, the inverse of `result`. In each stage's record the night's `SleepSession` gives its `SleepAction` as its `resultOf`, by its key alone, so the night has the same key there as in its own record.

**Steps and active energy are amounts over each sample's period.** A step count is the steps taken from its `startTime` to its `endTime`, and counts over a longer time add up. The watch and the phone each count steps, often over the same minutes, and each count is a reading of its own, so adding them all up counts those steps twice. On a real export, Health's daily step totals matched taking, in each 15 minutes of the clock, the readings of the device highest in Health's list of sources for steps. Choosing a device for each stretch of time is left to whatever reads them, as is which day a total is for.

**Heart rate variability is named for how it's measured.** Health's is SDNN, the standard deviation of the intervals between beats, almost always over a minute, so its property is `heartRateVariabilitySdnn`. Other sources measure it as RMSSD, which differs from SDNN over the same beats, so theirs would be a property of its own. SDNN also grows with the length of time it's over, and each reading keeps its `startTime` and `endTime`.

**A blood pressure is one reading.** Health pairs the two values in a correlation, and the export repeats each value as a sample of its own. The correlation gives one reading with both values, and the repeated samples are skipped.

**An ECG gives its rhythm.** Only the header of each recording in `electrocardiograms/` is read: when it was recorded, its classification, and the watch's hardware. A recording classified as a rhythm, `Sinus Rhythm` or `Atrial Fibrillation`, gives a `heartRhythm` reading. One that's inconclusive says nothing about the rhythm and is left out. The voltage samples, the symptoms, and the name and date of birth the file starts with aren't read. The recording names the watch's model but not the watch, so its only instrument is the model.

**Values are in the units the vocabulary defines.** Heart rates are in beats per minute, heart rate variability in milliseconds, blood oxygen as a fraction from 0 to 1, temperatures in degrees Celsius, weight in kilograms, blood pressure in millimeters of mercury, steps as a count, and active energy in kilocalories, which Health calls `Cal`. A sleeping wrist temperature in degrees Fahrenheit is converted, and so are a weight in pounds or stones, to the gram, and energy in kilojoules. A sample in a unit there's no conversion for is left out, and the run's summary says how many.

**You are your iCloud account.** The export doesn't say whose it is, so it's taken to be the iCloud account signed in on this Mac, from `@chronicle.app/apple`. An explicit `account` object describes someone else's export. With no account, or `account` set to null, you are Health's own record of you, keyed by `source` alone.

**Left out:** heart rate variability's beat-by-beat values, the motion context of a heart rate (sedentary or active), low and high heart rate notifications, workouts and their routes, activity rings, distance, basal energy, exercise and stand time, food, and the other types Health keeps. `export_cda.xml` is a clinical-format copy of the same data and isn't read.

## Tests

The tests run each kind over a synthetic export, written by the fixture, and pass an explicit iCloud account. They never read a real Health export or the host's iCloud account.
